The module¶

The ISF15ACP4 is a frameless pushbutton with a 96 RGB x 64 passive-matrix colour OLED in its cap. It has ten through-hole terminals: two for the SPST switch (pins 1 and 2) and eight for the display (pins 3 to 10). The display is driven by an SSD1331 of Solomon Systech: the NKK sheet does not name it, and NKK's application notes ("Part Numbers": all OLED modules incorporate the same OLED controller, SSD1331) do (Ambiguities). The SSD1331 datasheet used came from a mirror (the manufacturer's page lists the part and links no PDF): the documents, revisions and SHA-256 are in docs/datasheets.yml.

The module has no MISO pin: the serial interface is write only (SSD1331 section 7.1.3, NKK sheet E6). The driver never reads from the display, and Limitations states what follows from that.

The 15 V OLED supply is not on the module and is not the driver's: it is a regulator of yours with an enable pin. The driver switches it through the port's GpioSet(CtrlPin::VccEnable, ...) and never enables it before the controller is reset and configured. Give the enable a pull-down: it must be off while the MCU resets and boots, because the controller is unconfigured then and the OLED supply must not be on (Contract).

Control pins¶

The driver drives two pins through GpioSet() with logical levels, and the port maps them to the physical level:

CtrlPin GpioSignal::ACTIVE means Physical level Idle level, set before the first transfer
Reset RES# asserted, the controller held in reset low high (not in reset)
VccEnable the OLED supply on (14 V to 16 V on the ISF15ACP4, 15 V to 17 V on the others, Power) high (a port may invert it for a P-channel switch) low (supply off)

D/C# is set by the port's Write() for each transfer, from the DataKind it receives. CS# is low for the bytes of one transfer. SCK idles low in mode 0.

A transfer is one command (an opcode and its parameters, at most 33 bytes) or one run of at most 96 pixel bytes; the driver sets D/C# low for the first and high for the second.

Power order¶

State (NKK sheet E7) VDD VCC Controller Driver call
0 off off off before EnsureInitialized(), after Deinitialize()
1 on off reset, configured, RAM cleared, display off EnsureInitialized(), DisplayOff()
2 on on display on DisplayOn(), DisplayDim()

The datasheet's sequence (SSD1331 section 7.9) is: VDD on; RES# low for at least 3 µs, then high; VCC on at least 3 µs after RES# falls and after the initialisation commands; display on, 100 ms until the outputs are on. Power-down is display off, VCC off, and VDD off after tOFF (100 ms typical). The driver does the rest of the sequence and leaves VDD to you: cut it only after Deinitialize() has returned.

The NKK application notes agree and add three rules. VCC is disabled at power-up (Design Considerations 1 and Initialization); RES# is low for at least 3 µs and VCC may be enabled "any time from this point to before the display is turned ON at the end of initialization" (Initialization), so the driver's order (VCC after the configuration, display on after vcc_settle_ms) is inside what the notes allow, and the model's rule that the whole configuration precedes VCC is stricter than the notes. VCC can never be below VDD, because of the internal ESD diode from VDD to VCC: when VCC is disabled it is either at VDD or floating. And VCC must not be present after VDD is gone: make the VCC capacitance smaller than the VDD capacitance or add a pull-down on VCC, because the user can cut the power at any time without the turn-off procedure (Design Considerations 1, FAQ "Does the power up/down sequence have to be followed?").

The supplies differ by variant (NKK sheets E5, E9 and E11; absolute maximum 19 V VCC and 4.0 V VDD on all three):

Variant VDD VCC minimum / typical / maximum Drive current VCC, all pixels on (typical / maximum)
Isf15acp4 2.4 V to 3.5 V 14 V / 15 V / 16 V 11.0 mA / 13.2 mA
Isc15anp4 2.4 V to 3.5 V 15 V / 16 V / 17 V 3.8 mA / 4.6 mA
Isc01p 2.4 V to 3.5 V 15 V / 16 V / 17 V 2.4 mA / 2.9 mA

NKK's application notes (pin list, "VCC: Supply voltage for the OLED (15V to 17V)"; Design Considerations 1, "VCC (16V)") state one range for the whole family, 15 V to 17 V with a 16 V charge pump, and the VDD range 2.4 V to 3.5 V. The ISF15ACP4 sheet (E5) is narrower, 14 V to 16 V with 15 V typical, and NKK's charge-pump manual (Revision C, section 1) lists the ISF15ACP4 only with the 15 V IS-CHPMPHP, and the other two with either pump. The driver's tables keep the narrower per-part sheet range for the ISF15ACP4 (the part's own sheet is the safe side: 17 V is above its maximum), and the notes' 15 V to 17 V for the other two. 16 V is inside the range of all three and inside the notes' range; 15 V to 16 V is the common range. Set your regulator to the variant's own range.

NKK sells two charge pumps for this (notes, Part Numbers; the pump manual's own figures differ from the notes', so both are given): the IS-CHPMP, 16 V, 21 mA in the notes and 20 mA in the manual, and the IS-CHPMPHP, 100 mA in the notes, 15 V and 130 mA in the manual. Each has a shut-down input: logic high is normal operation, low shuts it down and leaves the output floating (manual, section 3; kit manual: enable high). The IS-CHPMPHP has a pull-down on that input; the IS-CHPMP has none, so a port driving VccEnable for it needs an external pull-down or the supply is undefined while the MCU pin floats. The ISF15ACP4 draws 11 mA typical and 13.2 mA at most (its sheet), which is why the manual lists only the IS-CHPMPHP for it.

The module's inputs must not exceed VDD + 0.3 V (NKK sheet E5, absolute maximum): a 3.3 V MCU on a module run at the typical 2.8 V violates it, so run the module and the MCU from the same rail (within 3.5 V) or level-shift.

DisplayOff() and Deinitialize() give the sleep state. Config::master_current (0 to 15) scales the segment current: lower it for less heat and a longer life.

Variants¶

Variant Pixels Switch RAM column of the first pixel
Isf15acp4 96 x 64 yes 0
Isc15anp4 64 x 48 yes 16
Isc01p 52 x 36 no 22

PresetFor(variant) returns the module's configuration. The pixel counts are on the NKK sheets. The column origins, the display offsets, the multiplex ratios and every initialisation value are the ones in NKK's application notes: Table 1 (ISC15ANP4), Table 2 (ISC01P) and Table 3 (ISF15ACP4), steps 4.1 to 4.18, and the memory-window commands of "Specifying a Memory Window" (columns 10h to 4Fh and 16h to 49h for the two smaller modules, 00h to 5Fh for the ISF15ACP4). tests/test_nkk_tables.cpp checks each number against the table. NKK Note 1: changing the set-up values (contrast, display offset, multiplex ratio, master configuration, phase periods, clock, linear gray scale, pre-charge level, VCOMH) may damage the OLED module. The setters exist for the datasheet's ranges, not for these values; change them only on a module you can afford to lose (Ambiguities).

The Isc01p has 8 pins, not 10, and no switch contacts. Its order differs from the table above (NKK sheet E11):

Pin 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
Name VDD SS RES D/C SCK SDI VCC GND

The Isc15anp4 has the same 10 pins as the ISF15ACP4. Do not wire an ISC01P from the 10-pin table: its pin 3 is RES and its pin 7 is VCC.
